About 1 minute 15. Two routes for the same USD 20,000. Provider A has the lower fee, 1,500 rupees against 3,000, so most people would pick A. But B wins: 18,23,080 rupees against 18,27,700, which is 4,620 rupees cheaper. The why breakdown shows exactly where that comes from: B's better exchange rate saves 6,000 rupees, A's fee is 1,500 lower, and because less is remitted through B, TCS is 120 rupees less. 6,000 minus 1,500 plus 120 is 4,620. The code writes these sentences, not the model, and they always add up. The bottom line is the hidden markup: compared to a mid-market rate of 90.00, Provider A's rate hides 10,000 rupees of cost that never appears as a fee. The 90.00 mid-market rate is an example value from our tests, not a live rate.

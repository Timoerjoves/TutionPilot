About 1 minute 15. Two worked examples. A2: a family pays 15 lakh rupees from its own money and has sent nothing else this year. The first 10 lakh is free of TCS, so 5 lakh is taxable, and 2 percent of 5 lakh is 10,000 rupees. If the same 15 lakh is paid with an education loan, scenario A3, TCS is zero. A8: the family pays USD 20,000 through Provider B at 90.20 rupees per dollar. That is 18,04,000 rupees. The part above 10 lakh is 8,04,000, and 2 percent of that is 16,080. Add the 3,000 fee and 18,23,080 rupees leave the account. Along the bottom: A1, A4, A5 and A6. A5 and A6 rely on rules we have not verified yet, so the product labels them. In A6 the agent asks for the PAN first before showing the 5 percent figure. Every example also assumes TCS is charged on the amount sent and not on fees; that is still unverified, and we say so.

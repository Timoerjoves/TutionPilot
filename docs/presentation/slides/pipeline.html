About 1 minute 15. Nishanth: please check this slide against the app before we submit. The app tells the payment as a guided six-step story. One, Welcome: the family gives two consents before any data is read. Two, Invoice: the screen animates the check of the university's digital signature, link by link. Three, Questions: the agent asks one thing at a time and never guesses a missing fact. Four, Best route: a chart of what each route costs on top of the real exchange value, what-if sliders that change the tax live, and a gauge for the yearly LRS limit. Five, Review and sign: the parent types a PIN and the parent's own device signs the exact plan. Six, Receipt: the transaction, the university's certificate, the parent's signature and the audit entry. Beside the story, a live agent panel shows every step and policy decision, the rule trace with sources, exactly what the AI saw after masking, and the audit chain. The Attack lab runs 16 attacks against a separate copy of the agent. A student can review but cannot sign. Behind the screens there are twelve pipeline stages. The design rule is: code computes, the model explains. The model only sees masked text, and every number it writes is checked against the engine. To be honest, the model today is an offline, deterministic stand-in; no hosted AI model is connected yet.
